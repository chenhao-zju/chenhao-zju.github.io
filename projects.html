--- layout: page title: Projects permalink: /projects/ --- I have participated in research and engineering projects across post-training, multi-modal AI, weather forecasting, and computer vision. The main projects are as follows:

Sparse MoE and Dense MoE @ Shanghai AI Laboratory (2024.09–2026.01)

Considering the requirements of different atmospheric scenarios, we designed dense MoE and sparse MoE structures for multi-variable injection and model acceleration, respectively. The variables-adaptive dense MoE improved multi-variable weather forecasting accuracy by 11.3% compared with a vanilla Transformer, while the sparse MoE acceleration framework reduced inference latency by 42% while retaining 98% of the original prediction performance. Related works include VA-MoE, STCast, and EMFormer.

SKNO (Huawei): Earlier at Huawei, we designed SKNO by integrating the KNO model and SHT operator for global weather forecasting on ERA5, with 6-hour temporal resolution and 1.4-degree spatial resolution. The related research has been open-sourced in [code].

Post-Training & Vision-Language Models (2026.02–Present)

At ByteDance (Jindouyun) and Tencent Youtu (Project Up), I work on general-purpose Vision-Language Models and post-training pipelines, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT), domain-specific instruction tuning, and on-policy distillation (OPD) for risk-control and multi-turn reasoning scenarios.

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in LLM @ iFlytek: Previously, I was responsible for supervised fine-tuning in long-text scenarios. We adopted a divide-and-conquer strategy: segment long documents, summarize each part, and then mine useful information holistically.

Few-Shot Segmentation @ Zhongfei Zhang (IEEE Fellow) (2022.04–2023.06)

MCINet : We design a model to fuse different contents from a learnable branch and a frozen backbone, and achieve SOTA on 8 standard benchmarks for PASCAL and COCO with ResNet50 and ResNet101. The related work has been accepted by TOMM.

DAM and PMNet : Further explore lightweight few-shot segmentation models. With only 0.68M parameters, the model shows strong performance across cross-class, cross-dataset, and cross-domain settings. Related works have been accepted by WACV 2024 and Neurocomputing.

Self-Prompt Learning (2023.04–2023.10)

SPPEL : Different from general prompt learning with extra-prompt support, we design a model that obtains prompt support from its own input. The related work has been accepted by TCSVT.
